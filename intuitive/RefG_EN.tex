\section*{Introduction}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Introduction}

Modern physics resembles an intricate puzzle. Its individual pieces have been calculated with extraordinary precision and tested experimentally, yet they still resist assembly into a single physical picture. The Standard Model of particle physics and the geometrical description of gravity do not fit fully within one language. In the interiors of black holes, classical geometry reaches singularities; standard cosmology explains galactic rotation curves and the regularities of modified Newtonian dynamics (MOND) through dark-matter halos, and the accelerated expansion of the Universe through dark energy.

Quantum randomness, the nature of time, the hierarchy of particle masses, the fundamental constants, and the relative weakness of gravity belong to the same larger picture. Each has been studied in depth, but their common physical basis remains unseen.

The history of physics offers many attempts to bring these problems together. Albert Einstein sought the answer in the geometry of spacetime, while John Archibald Wheeler connected geometry and information through the principle of \textit{It from Bit}. Andrei Sakharov described gravity as an effect induced by quantum fluctuations of the vacuum; Ted Jacobson derived it from the thermodynamics of horizons; and Erik Verlinde interpreted it as an entropic and holographic phenomenon. Roger Penrose treats spacetime, cosmology, and quantum physics as a single geometrical problem.

Their mechanisms differ, but the central question is shared: can many phenomena be described from one foundation?

This paper assembles ideas developed over many years into a unified logical structure and presents the ontological framework of \textbf{Refractive Gravity (RefG)}.

Modern physics generally begins with coordinates, time, and fields already in place. RefG steps back and asks: before speaking of space, time, or mass, what creates a stable and measurable distinction in nature?

In 1905, Einstein abandoned absolute time and defined simultaneity operationally through clocks and light signals \citep{einstein1905}. RefG poses that operational question at an earlier level: what physical condition creates the clock and the signal on which those operations themselves depend?

The argument rests on three fundamental principles:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{First:} Nature has only one ontological foundation---one primary identity. Spatial location, clock time, the measurable Universe, and the physically manifested medium arise through its resonant self-distinction. Fields, forces, and particles are phase, pressure, shear, rotational, or topological modes of that manifested medium.
\item \textbf{Second:} The stable unit of matter is the \emph{oscillon}---a locally confined, self-sustaining resonant form of the manifested medium. Its intense internal dynamics leave a persistent pressure-deficit imprint in the surrounding medium.
\item \textbf{Third:} Gravity is the refractive response of the manifested medium. The oscillon's oscillatory action propagates through the medium, lowers its static pressure, and creates a long-range deficit imprint. The stable profile of this mechanism, phase-locked to its source, is read as ordinary gravity; the coherent component released during a rapid reconfiguration of the source is observed as a gravitational wave. Attraction is motion within an effective geometry altered by an oscillation-generated pressure deficit.
\end{itemize}

The Michelson--Morley experiment of 1887 found no ether wind of the kind expected from the Earth's motion and placed stringent constraints on a stationary ether independent of matter \citep{michelson1887}. RefG proposes a different picture: space, time, the measuring instrument, and the observer are all modes of a medium generated from one foundation.

Under uniform motion, form is transmitted with wave and phase symmetry. On the Lorentz-covariant branch, this process leaves no trace of energy loss. Lord Kelvin's intuition is extended here: matter is itself a form of the medium, while the measurable Universe takes shape through the self-distinction of the primary foundation.

This text brings the calculations collected in the public Zenodo archive into an intuitive physical picture \citep{lotishvili2026a,lotishvili2026b}. It connects the self-distinction of the foundation with measurability, the pressure deficit of mass with cosmological horizons, and the origin of particles with the topology of quantum numbers. Earlier programs also pursued medium-based unification, including Winterberg's Planck aether \citep{winterberg2002}. RefG places its central emphasis on the physical origin of measurability and the common resonant locking of the particle population. Its principal contributions are as follows:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Population-locked time:} Self-distinction first produces a pre-clock ordering of events; mutual locking across the particle population then turns that order into a local clock rate. Each mode follows the shared rhythm through its own stable ratio. Time dilation is a collective reconfiguration of this local network. This population-based definition of temporal rate is a distinctive element of RefG.
\item \textbf{Two-channel accounting:} Gravitational mass is, by definition, a readout channel through the medium. The oscillon's internal state is retained as a physically separate quantity, and RefG distinguishes the two axiomatically.
\item \textbf{Separation of size-dependent and local properties:} On the selected first-order biconformal branch, size, externally read mass, and clock rate follow the pressure change directly, whereas the coordinate propagation of light follows it quadratically. RefG relates this distinction to the different roles of nodes and the connections between them.
\item \textbf{The Koide three-phase structure:} In the three-phase map of the charged leptons, the second branch of the ninth-order return cycle fixes the phase at two ninths of a radian, while equalization of the internal channels fixes the modulation depth at the square root of two.
\item \textbf{A node-based interpretation of the fine-structure constant:} In the RefG picture, the fine-structure constant is a dimensionless, invariant ratio between the electric and magnetic responses of the node network of the manifested medium. It describes the relative strength of the smallest electric flux and the transverse oscillation of the photon supported by the same network.
\item \textbf{The scope of process time:} Coordinate time and process time are clearly distinguished. Process time serves as an explanatory language for physical phenomena, while cosmological observations are calculated in coordinate time.
\item \textbf{Two readouts of one operator:} RefG's unified spectral map associates short-wavelength modes with the particle readout and long-wavelength modes with the cosmic-web readout.
\end{enumerate}

The novelty of each link in this chain must be assessed separately. Medium-based unification programs have precedents, and a similar separation between linear and quadratic dependences also appears in polarizable-vacuum models \citep{dicke1957,puthoff2002}. RefG derives these two powers from the distinct roles of nodes and connections and joins them to two-channel mass accounting.

The three-phase parametrization of the Koide relation and its geometrical reading continue the work of Brannen and Foot, while Shulga developed a different holonomy-based derivation of the phase corresponding to two ninths of a radian \citep{brannen2010,foot1994,shulga2026}. RefG connects this structure to phase indistinguishability, the ninth-order return cycle, and equalization of the internal channels.

The relaxation-based reading of dark energy and the knot-based argument for three-dimensionality likewise draw on established lines of thought \citep{klinkhamer2008,whitrow1955,berera2017}. The distinctive backbone of this work is their connection to the birth of measurability, a population-wide shared temporal rate, two-channel accounting, and the two scale-dependent readouts of a single spectral operator.

In this picture, dark energy is associated with the remnant of the historical relaxation of the self-distinguished cosmological background, while three-dimensionality is associated with knot closure and topological stability.

Modern information, symbolic, and software tools support the systematic computational testing of the intuitive ideas developed in this research.

The text invites the reader to follow this logical chain and see how a single physical picture connects phenomena that otherwise appear separate. The scientific value of the framework will be determined by its further formalization and observational testing.

\section{Ontological Foundation}

\subsection{One Foundation and Its Manifested Medium: Substance, Emptiness, and the Dynamics of Existence}

Modern fundamental physics describes the universe through several independent fields. In the Standard Model, each field has its own dynamics and rules of interaction. RefG begins instead from a single principle: there is one ontological foundation---one primary identity---whose resonant self-distinction gives rise to a multimodal physical medium. Fields and forces are phase, pressure, shear, rotational, and topological modes of this common medium.

The idea of a unified foundation has a long history in physics. William Clifford pictured matter as small, moving \emph{hills} of spatial curvature \citep{clifford1876}. Clifford's picture was purely geometric; the medium of RefG carries pressure, shear, and resonant channels together with its geometric response.

A physical example of distinct particle modes emerging from one carrier is provided by Levin and Wen's \emph{string-net} condensation model. Within the same bosonic system, collective excitations arise as gauge bosons and fermions and, in suitable phases, behave like photons, electrons, gluons, and quarks \citep{levinwen2005}. Their model begins with an already structured quantum system. RefG associates the same possibility with the self-distinction of a single pre-spatial foundation.

The simplest dynamical picture of the manifested medium lies between two limiting states. At one limit is maximal substance, where pressure, mass--energy potential, and operational density are extremely high. At the other is the zero limit, where interaction and measurability disappear. Physical reality is the bounded resonant dynamics between these limits. \emph{Nodes of existence} arise within this dynamics.

One foundation unfolds into many properties in the manifested medium. The medium can be compressed, twisted, set into vortical flow, and made to carry a wave. Water makes the idea intuitive: one fluid simultaneously supports pressure, flow, and surface tension, although these properties are not interchangeable. In the same way, the RefG medium combines phase, pressure, shear, and topological responses. This multichannel structure underlies the formation of electric charge, spin, and the particle spectrum.

The manifested medium has a quiescent background state, denoted formally by $\varphi=0$. Every measurable location is defined by relations among nodes of the self-distinguished medium. The local excitation level, the operational density of nodes, and the strength of their connections may all vary. In this language, a pressure drop, or rarefaction, is an increase in the interval between nodes that cannot be measured directly.

Here \emph{pressure} means the effective pressure--energy state of the medium. Pressure rises above the background, while below it a deficit branch appears. Gravity in this paper is read through that deficit profile. Classical fluid pressure is its intuitive analogue. From outside, a change in the deficit appears as a change in clock rate, signal propagation, operational size, and effective mass.
\subsection{From One Existence to Multiplicity: The Origin of Measurability and Space}

A completely undifferentiated foundation is one indivisible primary identity. Its resonant self-distinction establishes the first distinguishable relation; at that same threshold, location, clock-measurable duration, quantity, and counting arise.

In the undifferentiated state, this identity retains its full unity. Place, size, mass, clock duration, beginning, and end are properties of self-distinguished processes and arise with them.

Measurable physics begins with resonant self-distinction. At the first stage, resonance is a self-consistent, closed relation among states of the foundation. This pre-clock structure establishes an ordering of events; comparison among stable cycles then gives rise to clock time.

Before self-distinction, this origin is called the \textbf{primary identity} or the \textbf{foundation}. A persistent difference generated through self-distinction takes the form of a node, a phase displacement, or a pressure deficit. A \textbf{node} is a distinguishable state of the one foundation; an \textbf{imprint} is the change left by that state; and a \textbf{relation} is the comparison of one state with another. A multiplicity of nodes consists of mutually distinguishable manifestations of one identity, held together as a whole by a common resonant order.

Quantity begins at this threshold. A difference that can be read persistently produces countable physical units. Manifestations become distinguishable from one another, retain their own imprints within the common order, and can therefore be counted.

The primary unit of measurement is not an isolated object but a distinguishable relation. A completely undifferentiated identity contains no second term of comparison. Resonant self-distinction produces two mutually distinguishable states of the same foundation and the first connection between them; counting begins at precisely this point. The foundation does not break into separate pieces. It retains its unity, while its persistent, mutually distinguishable manifestations are read as multiplicity.

In informational language, the first preserved difference is analogous to a bit. A distinguishable manifestation is read as \texttt{1}, while the absence of its imprint in a given relation is read as \texttt{0}. Different arrangements of these signs gradually form more complex structures, just as mutually distinguishable states of the foundation generate the diversity of the measurable universe.

The relation between two distinguishable nodes is the physical seed of space. Delay in their connection is the seed of time; their extension across the network gives rise to operational size; and the energetic tension required to preserve the difference provides the internal basis of mass. External gravitational mass is read from the deficit profile that this tension creates in the medium.

Thus the resonant self-distinction of one primary identity produces distinguishable states. Their preserved differences create quantity, while their mutual relations create space. The relational ordering of changes establishes a pre-clock sequence, and comparison among stable cycles produces clock time. Extension through the network determines size; stable energetic confinement and its deficit imprint determine internal mass and mass as read from outside.

This view also clarifies the nature of the observer. We and our measuring instruments are complex structures built from the same nodal network. The idea echoes Bridgman's operationalism, according to which a physical concept is defined by the operations through which it is measured \citep{bridgman1927}. RefG asks the deeper question: what physical condition makes measurement itself possible?

In this picture, the universe is a continuous physical process generated by the self-distinction of one foundation. The foundation has no separate spatial location, because place itself arises only from relations among its mutually distinguishable manifestations. Every measurable location is a different manifestation of this one identity.

The observer and the measuring apparatus belong to the same medium, so the interval between nodes is read through its own physical imprint. A distinguishable relation between two nodes produces space. Below that threshold, the difference remains an internal state of the medium; beyond it, the difference appears as a change in time, size, mass, and signal propagation.

RefG posits a minimum threshold of operational distinguishability, denoted by $\ell_*$. Its physical content is the formation of a relation preserved as an independent, stable imprint. Below this threshold, spatial separation does not exist as a measurable property. At the threshold, the first operational interval is born; above it, that interval is read through the signatures of pressure, clock rate, and signal propagation. The Planck length is a natural physical scale, while $\ell_*$ is the hypothetical threshold for the formation of a stable record. The microdynamics of the medium must determine whether the two coincide.

The operational basis of the measurable universe is therefore the first stable relation that makes multiplicity possible. The foundation is one; the universe is the network of its mutually distinguishable nodes and connections.

The same object occupying different locations at different times provides a simple image of this idea. If its entire path is shown in a single picture with the time labels removed, the result is an extended trace: the identity is one, while the marked locations are many. RefG takes this picture to a deeper level. Persistent self-distinctions of the foundation create the very locations that can then be counted and compared.

A related idea appears in Mohrhoff's quantum ontology, where spatial extension and particle multiplicity arise from one non-spatial and atemporal origin. In that picture, space is constituted by reflexive relations, and fundamental particles are read as numerically identical with the same underlying entity \citep{mohrhoff2014}. RefG joins this motif to the hypothesis of resonant self-distinction. The transition must generate a medium, clock time, and mutually distinguishable modes whose energy, momentum, and charge are additive.

The atemporal status of the foundation and the age of the measurable universe belong to different conceptual levels. Age belongs to the self-distinguished regime; the completely undifferentiated foundation-state is atemporal.

Mutual locking among stable oscillons turns the pre-clock sequence into a clock-measurable rate.

At this boundary, the primary physical content of gravity also appears. When the connection between nodes weakens, it is read from outside as a fall in pressure, a slowing of clocks, and a change in signal propagation. At macroscopic scales, this coordinated change is called a gravitational field.
\subsection{Nodal Transmission: Waves, Time, and Self-Calibration}

Self-distinction of the foundation gives rise to a unified network of nodes of existence. Each node is a functionally distinct state of the same foundation: it preserves an imprint of change and transmits it through the network. The operational density of nodes describes how closely these manifestations can be resolved. At the first level, adjacency is direct physical coupling; stable order among such couplings gives rise to spatial proximity and operational distance.

A wave is the sequential transmission of state and phase through this network. Light is a freely propagating transverse phase imprint, whereas an oscillon is a locally confined, self-sustaining pattern of the same transmission. As matter moves, this confined form is relayed from node to node and re-establishes the same phase coherence at each new location. Light and matter are therefore propagating and confined modes of one medium.

Every transmission establishes a relational sequence of events. Repeated phase relations organize this sequence into a pre-clock order, while comparison among stable oscillon cycles turns it into clock time. The local rate of time is the collective rhythm of the entire network: it records both the rate at which the medium transmits state and the rate at which an oscillon completes its cycle.

Thermal insulation in double glazing offers a clear analogy. Evacuating the gas between the panes suppresses convection and, at sufficiently low pressure, also reduces molecular thermal conduction, although thermal radiation still propagates. The essential point is that less heat is transferred per unit time. RefG extends this intuition to the nodal medium and relates the rate of transmission to its density and coupling strength.

Imagine an observer whose body, clock, and ruler are themselves made from this flow of heat. Such an observer could not isolate the carrier gas using a standard of measurement independent of the observer's own physical mode; the gas would be inferred only from changes in pressure and heat transfer. In RefG, the observer, the measuring apparatus, light, and matter are all modes of one manifested medium. We therefore read the state of the foundation only through its collective pressure imprint---through changes in clock rate, signal propagation, operational size, and effective mass.

In a deficit region, the operational density of nodes falls, pressure decreases, and the common rate of nodal transmission slows. A distant observer reads this single change as slower wave propagation, longer oscillon cycles, reduced operational size, and an altered effective-mass readout. The local observer's clock, ruler, body, and light signal are reconfigured with the same medium; in local units, the observer continues to measure the usual rate, the usual size, and the same invariant local speed of light.

The same confined resonant pattern preserves its topological identity and internal phase relations in both dense and rarefied nodal configurations. What changes is its realization in the medium---how broadly it extends across the operational network and what mass is read from a distance. The local ruler belongs to the same configuration and therefore cannot register a common change of scale relative to itself. Comparing processes that have traversed different environments, however, reveals the difference as a real physical change in time, size, and mass.

The operational density of nodes, coupling strength, and transmission rate are interdependent properties of one medium. In a deficit region they vary coherently and together define a unified pressure-mediated dynamics. On the physically admissible low-energy branch, transmission must be isotropic, lossless, and Lorentz symmetric: the local speed of light remains invariant, and uniform motion leaves no trace of drag.

\subsection{The Shared Local Rhythm and Resonant Locking}

The shared rhythm of local time emerges through mutual locking among stable oscillons---the wave forms of matter. Within a unified resonant network, their internal frequencies settle into fixed, stable ratios, while the internal coordination of the local network establishes the rate of time.

No physical frequency can be assigned to the undifferentiated foundation; a vibrational spectrum forms only in the self-distinguished medium. This three-dimensional oscillatory medium sets the boundaries and determines which modes can exist. The sound of a bell likewise arises from modes resonating together across the whole body. In the same way, the frequencies of elementary particles are established through mutual balance in a common medium. The medium's microdynamics selects the admissible modes, and their mutual coordination produces a stable, locked configuration.

Every particle mode has its own frequency, yet all follow the same local rhythm and preserve stable ratios to one another. These ratios need not correspond to integer harmonics; the resulting spectrum instead resembles that of a three-dimensional, mildly anharmonic resonator. Each mode retains its identity, while a rhythm incompatible with the network loses phase coherence and cannot persist as a stable particle form.

In a low-pressure region, the shared rate of the entire local resonant network changes. A dense nodal configuration corresponds to strong effective coupling and a faster common rhythm; in a rarefied medium, the collective transmission rate falls. The operational density of nodes, coupling strength, and clock rate are interrelated properties of one medium, whose joint effect is expressed by its pressure state.

All local clocks and particle processes follow a change in the common rate together. A local observer continues to read time as usual, while the network's internal dimensionless ratios---including the fine-structure constant and particle-mass ratios---remain invariant.

The classical precursor of this relational view is Ernst Mach's principle \citep{mach1883}. RefG gives it a local form: the common rhythm is set directly by the local resonant network, and physical action therefore remains local.

In the RefG map, the fine-structure constant is the dimensionless, invariant ratio between the electric and magnetic responses of the nodal network.

As a wave passes from one pressure regime into another, it adjusts to the new medium. Its observed frequency is determined by the effective metric. In RefG language, comparison between the local rhythms of source and receiver is a translation of phase; cosmological redshift is read from the ratio between the scales at emission and reception.

A wave does not carry a frozen, absolute frequency along its path. In each region, its phase adjusts to the common local rhythm. A compatible imprint locks into the new resonant network; an incompatible one loses phase coherence or scatters, and its energy passes into other compatible channels of the medium.

The rate of time is therefore the physical state of the local resonant network. When pressure changes, the entire network reconfigures collectively: all compatible frequencies change together, while their dimensionless ratios remain invariant. The validity of the common reconfiguration is tested by this invariance.
\subsection{The Oscillon, the Pressure Deficit, and the Origin of Mass}

In this framework, electrons, protons, and other particles are treated as oscillons. An oscillon is a stable wave structure confined within the medium---a highly organized form that sustains itself. The historical precursor of this idea is Lord Kelvin's vortex-atom model \citep{thomson1867}. That particular program belongs to history, but its central intuition remains: a particle may be a stable, dynamical form of the medium itself. RefG develops this intuition in modern resonant and topological language.

The confined form of an oscillon is local, but its oscillatory action does not stop at its local boundary. Each cycle transmits a phase and pressure change to neighboring nodes; the imprint propagates from node to node and spreads throughout the connected, boundaryless medium. As it covers an ever larger region, its amplitude decreases with distance.

When an oscillon oscillates or rotates intensely, or confines energy within its form, the dynamical activity of the medium increases and its static pressure falls. Bernoulli's picture makes the direction intuitive \citep{bernoulli1738}: greater dynamical intensity is accompanied by lower static pressure. On the selected weak branch of RefG, this analogy corresponds to a pressure--energy balance identity. During the formation or reconfiguration of a source, RefG relates the broad redistribution of oscillatory energy to relaxation of the cosmological background; Chapter III develops the thermodynamic content of this connection.

In RefG, ordinary gravity and a gravitational wave are two regimes of the same oscillatory mechanism of the medium. Ordinary gravity is a persistent wave profile phase-locked to its source: the oscillation extends across the entire connected network, but once the profile is stationary it no longer produces a net outward flux of energy. A gravitational wave is the coherent oscillatory change released from that profile when the source reconfigures rapidly, carrying energy outward. An ordinary wave and a tsunami share the same ocean and propagation mechanism; they differ in regime, scale, coherence, and transported energy. Observations of gravitational waves establish that a propagating disturbance periodically changes the separation between freely falling test bodies. RefG reads this large traveling wave as the outgoing manifestation of the same channel whose stationary profile constitutes ordinary gravity.

Through its active existence, an oscillon creates a pressure-deficit profile in the manifested medium. In this map, the deficit is the direct external profile of gravitational mass: its integral readout yields the system's gravitational charge. The more intense the oscillon's internal dynamics, the deeper the deficit and the stronger the refractive gradient. The oscillon's internal reserve is accounted for separately from this external readout.

The local depth of the deficit and the total mass read from a great distance are not the same quantity. The first describes the central gradient and the state of compactness; the second combines the active core, the dressing supplied by the medium, and the negative contribution from binding. After a merger, the local deficit may therefore deepen even as the total mass read from afar decreases by the energy carried away in radiation.

This view has a well-known parallel in the history of physics: John Archibald Wheeler's program of \emph{mass without mass}, in which a geon is a massive configuration held together by the energy of its own field \citep{wheeler1955}. Wheeler's geons gradually radiate their energy away; RefG associates oscillon stability with resonant locking and topological protection.

The term \emph{effective refractive mass} means the mass of one oscillon as read from outside in a given environment. Its internal state is determined by its topological class, phase relations, and intrinsic energy. When the background changes slowly, this internal structure preserves its identity, while the pressure of the medium changes the oscillon's operational size and externally read mass. The total mass of the complete system as read from afar is determined by the full deficit profile. Externally read mass is therefore determined by the medium's refractive response.

At this fundamental level, gravity is the refractive deflection of the wave form of one oscillon within the pressure deficit created by another. A slack string provides an intuitive image of a gravitational well: the locally reduced pressure--energy state of the manifested medium weakens the conditions of propagation. The deficit profile shapes the effective geometry of the medium, and the particle's wave form moves refractively through that geometry.

An oscillon creates a pressure deficit, and the deficit in turn changes its effective mass, operational size, and coupling to the medium. RefG reads this two-way feedback as a mechanism of dynamical equilibrium: the material form changes the medium, and the altered medium determines the conditions of the form's external physical manifestation.
\section{Gravity as Refraction}

\subsection{Three Concurrent Operational Changes}

The pressure state of the manifested medium falls locally around an oscillon. In the nodal description, this corresponds to a lower density of nodes and weaker physical coupling. An external macroscopic observer reads this unified physical state through three coordinated changes.

\noindent\textbf{First: a reduction in the coordinate speed of light.} Conditions of collective transmission change within a pressure-deficit region, so light and other wave signals propagate more slowly in the coordinate description. The locally measured speed of light remains invariant; what changes is the rate read in the coordinate language of a distant observer.

\noindent\textbf{Second: a reduction in the operational size of the oscillon.} The oscillon preserves its fundamental topological form, while a rarefied medium entails reduced active participation within the nodal configuration. In the external description, this change appears as a reduced operational size and an altered effective-mass readout.

\noindent\textbf{Third: a reduction in the rate of the local resonant clock.} A clock is a real system built from oscillons, and its cadence depends on local nodal connections. In a rarefied medium, this coupling weakens and the clock slows relative to the coordinate time of a distant observer. The local clock continues to count its own cadence as usual.

These three changes are three operational readouts of a reduced-pressure state. They do not all depend on the change in the medium in the same quantitative way.

\subsection{The Quadratic Relation and the Factor of Two}

The three operational changes do not develop with equal strength. On the selected weak-field branch of RefG, operational size, the effective mass of an individual oscillon, and the rate of a local clock follow the change in pressure directly. In the working map of the same branch, the coordinate propagation of light follows the joint change in node density and coupling. The response is therefore quadratic, and a factor of two appears.

Similar powers also occur in polarizable-vacuum models \citep{dicke1957,puthoff2002}. RefG relates them to the ontological distinction between nodes and connections.

As the deficit deepens, the operational size and externally read effective mass of an individual oscillon decrease. The total gravitational mass of the system generating the deficit, by contrast, is the integral readout of the entire profile at large distance. Metric filters on the selected biconformal branch connect these two scales.

Four quantities must be kept distinct:

\begin{enumerate}
\item the oscillon's internal state and intrinsic energy;
\item the externally read effective mass of an individual oscillon in a given background;
\item the profile of the deficit source created in the manifested medium;
\item the total gravitational mass of the complete system as read from a great distance.
\end{enumerate}

These four levels form two balances. One part of the internal reserve is visible in the external readout, while another remains within strong confinement. The external readout combines the active core with the deficit layer formed around it---the medium-borne dressing. At large distances, both appear as one gravitational mass. The local energy of a test body and the mass of the complete system are therefore quantities read from the same configuration at different scales.

A colored, ice-like crystal immersed in clear water offers a vivid image of this self-consistent dynamical process. The crystal is a locally bound form of the same water, just as an oscillon is a local resonance of the common manifested medium. As the configuration forms, transforms, and relaxes, part of its energy passes from local confinement into a state distributed broadly through the medium---as the color of a dissolving crystal spreads through the water. Energy is redistributed among different channels of the system.

When the shape of a source changes rapidly---during nonuniform acceleration, merger, or relaxation---many oscillatory contributions combine coherently and generate a strong outgoing wave.
